# Competitor and Alternative Pages

Create comparison content that helps buyers make informed decisions while accurately representing every product. The objective is useful decision support, not misleading claims or keyword stuffing.

## Initial assessment

Establish your product's target users, actual capabilities, limitations, integrations, support, and current pricing. Identify relevant competitors, audience needs, page purpose, and primary evidence sources.

Do not invent features, prices, customer quotes, performance results, or market share.

## Page formats

### Single alternative page

- Why buyers evaluate alternatives.
- Product summary and fit.
- Key differences supported by evidence.
- Strengths and limitations of both products.
- Best-fit scenarios and reasons not to switch.
- Migration considerations and next steps.

### Alternatives roundup

- Selection criteria and methodology.
- Relevant shortlist of alternatives.
- Consistent summaries for each product.
- Comparison table with sourced claims.
- Recommendations by use case, budget, and team profile.
- Limitations and a transparent update date.

Include only real, relevant, researched products. Do not pad lists to reach an arbitrary count.

### Your product versus a competitor

- Concise summary of main differences.
- Side-by-side comparison by meaningful criteria.
- Features, integrations, usability, support, and pricing.
- Who should choose each product.
- Migration details, sources, and last-verified date.

### Competitor versus competitor

Use an impartial structure when comparing two third-party products. Explain the methodology and do not disguise promotional claims as independent research.

## Research and verification

For each product collect official URLs, verified capabilities and limitations, current plan pricing, integrations, deployment options, security statements, support terms, recent changes, and evidence links.

Use primary sources for product claims. Treat reviews as qualitative signals rather than definitive proof. Distinguish sourced facts, reported experiences, and analysis.

Pricing and feature availability change. Recheck before publication and show the verification date. Never extrapolate an unlisted price or SLA.

## Modular content architecture

For multi-page programs, centralize verified facts and source links. Track product identity, capability evidence, pricing dates, audience fit, and uncertainty. Avoid propagating unverified claims.

## SEO and content quality

Match the page to search intent. Use descriptive titles, headings, and internal links naturally. Do not generate near-duplicate pages by swapping product names. Add structured data only when page content meets the applicable schema requirements. Include original analysis and meaningful distinctions.

## Output

Provide URL and title, audience and search intent, methodology, draft or outline, comparison table where useful, sources and verification date, and open facts requiring owner confirmation.

## Editorial standard

Be fair about competitor strengths and your product's limitations. Never fabricate social proof, quote customer reviews without permission, or imply independent endorsement for promotional content.
